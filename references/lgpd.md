# LGPD decisions

Before describing something as personal data, examine whether it concerns an identified or identifiable natural person and its context. A legal entity's CNPJ is not automatically personal data; individual entrepreneurs' or representatives' data may be. Photos or voices are not automatically sensitive biometric data: analyze purpose and processing. Pseudonymization is not anonymization.

1. Delimit the operation, purpose and territorial scope (Articles 3–6).
2. Assess legal bases and requirements; consult [legal bases](legal-bases.md). Separate Articles 7, 11 and 14.
3. Identify who decides purpose/essential means and who follows instructions. The same SaaS can have different roles for billing and customer data.
4. Describe adequate information, channels and exercising rights (Articles 9, 18–20). Do not require excessive identity documentation.
5. Record operations (Article 37), impact-assessment needs (RIPD, Article 38), instructions, the data protection officer and applicable specific rules (Articles 39–41).
6. Compare security and incident-response practices with Articles 46–50 and effective regulations.

Retention needs criteria per operation. Closing accounts, deleting active data, expiring backups, fulfilling preservation obligations and anonymizing are distinct acts. Articles 15–16 do not authorize keeping everything indefinitely.

Do not claim encryption, authentication, a processor contract or a privacy page alone ensures compliance. For relevant automated decisions, confirm Article 20 applicability, how data subjects request review/information and the applicable limits. Do not invent a universal right to a particular review method without checking current law.
